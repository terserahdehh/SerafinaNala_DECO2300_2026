# Player-facing cymbal tolerance

20 October 2026. User chose a player-facing hit-zone extension with unchanged visuals. No APK built or installed; no commit or push.

The connected Quest was verified on version code 12 before this change. The saved scene places the nearest cymbal contact approximately 1.170 m from the player head/seat target; the other drum trigger volumes begin roughly 0.35–0.64 m away. This is scene geometry, not measured hand reach. The user's physical test found the high right cymbal unreachable/unresponsive. Reach is a supported concern, not an on-device trace proving the cause of every miss.

Only MusicRoom's CymbalHitZone MeshCollider mesh reference changes. The new Assets/Physics/CymbalReachContactMesh.asset preserves the original cymbal contact geometry and extends its convex hull toward the player in a narrow corridor. The original CymbalContactMesh asset is retained. The visible drum-kit mesh, collider transform, clip, DrumSound, all other collider settings and all gameplay scripts stay unchanged.

The new corridor is based on a 0.20 m wide, 0.16 m high, 0.56 m long box, oriented horizontally toward the seat; its centre is 0.27 m toward the player, 0.08 m toward world +X (away from mid-tom), and 0.04 m down from the original cymbal origin. Its eight vertices are merged into the original contact mesh; the existing collider cooks one connected convex hull. The closest contact is approximately 0.811 m from the seat target. No second collider or runtime trigger is added, so moving between the disc and assisted area does not cross an additional trigger boundary.

An initial wider candidate overlapped the mid-tom and was rejected in the isolated project. The saved narrow hull does not overlap any other drum trigger in the actual scene. Neighboring boxes were also temporarily inflated by 48 mm in total (24 mm per face), and remained disjoint. This conservatively checks clearance for a 12 mm-radius tip, so the same spherical tip cannot bridge both adjacent volumes simultaneously. It does not promise that a broad physical stroke cannot subsequently strike another instrument.

The unchanged original OnTriggerEnter/100 ms per-stick cooldown remains the only live hit path. No sweep, new velocity threshold, audio timer or proximity-based sound fallback was introduced. A player tip must enter the physical cymbal collider, including the explicitly authorised player-facing tolerance region, to produce sound. The extension means audible contact can occur before the tip touches the visual cymbal; this is the chosen accessibility tradeoff.

Validation is retained under Validation/CymbalReach. Unity cooked the convex hull successfully; all five neighboring drum volumes passed overlap and tip-clearance checks. 47 runtime assertions passed, including five assisted contacts per stick producing exactly one cymbal event each, plus all seven sounds and replay. Tests exercise the scene's original player tips, real physics trigger callbacks, original DrumSound, recording and replay. Nonzero listener DSP output is local audio-engine evidence, not physical Quest audibility. There were no production C# changes. Hash comparison verifies other scene blocks, scripts, settings and original assets are unchanged.
